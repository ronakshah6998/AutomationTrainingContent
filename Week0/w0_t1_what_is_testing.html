What is Software Testing & Why It Matters

Before we write a single line of code, let's understand what testing actually is — and why companies spend millions on it.

🔍
What is software testing?

Software testing is the process of checking whether a software application works as expected — and finding where it doesn't — before real users experience it.

Think of it this way. Before a car leaves the factory, engineers test the brakes, the engine, the lights. They don't wait for a customer to crash to discover the brakes don't work. Software testing is the same idea.

🚗
Real world
A mechanic tests brakes before delivering the car to the customer.
💻
Software world
A tester checks the login button before the app reaches real users.

Testing here: Review requirements for gaps and ambiguities before anything is built.
💰
Why find bugs early? The cost of waiting.

This is the most important concept in testing. The later a bug is found, the more expensive it is to fix. Not a little more — dramatically more.

← relative cost to fix a bug found at this stage

A bug found during requirements review might take 10 minutes to fix (just update a document). The same bug found in production could take weeks of hotfixes, rollbacks, and customer support — and may have already cost real money.

🌍
Real bugs that reached production — and what they cost
🚀
NASA Mars Orbiter
$327M lost
One team used metric units, another used imperial. The mismatch was never caught in testing. The spacecraft was lost.
🏦
Knight Capital
$440M in 45 min
A faulty software deployment caused the trading firm to lose $440 million in under an hour. The company nearly collapsed.
🏥
Therac-25 (Medical)
Lives lost
A radiation therapy machine had a software bug that delivered lethal doses of radiation. Patients died. No proper testing was done.
🛒
Amazon Pricing Bug
~$24M exposure
A pricing algorithm bug made thousands of products available for $0. Orders flooded in before the bug was caught.
✅
What a tester's job actually is
Find bugs before users do. A bug found by a tester costs the company minutes. A bug found by a user costs reputation, money, and trust.
Verify the software does what it's supposed to. Does the login page actually log you in? Does the payment actually charge correctly?
Catch what developers missed. Developers focus on building — testers focus on breaking. Both are essential.
Give confidence to release. Without testing, every release is a gamble. With testing, the team knows what works.
💡
One line to remember: Software testing is the safety net between what developers build and what users experience. Your job as a tester is to find the holes in that net before anyone falls through.
